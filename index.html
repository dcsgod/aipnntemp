What If Your Language Model Had a Thermostat?

Using statistical mechanics to build a physics-informed dynamic temperature controller for LLM decoding — with working code and benchmarks.

May 2025 ~18 min read GPT-2 · PyTorch · Physics-Informed NN

Every language model you've ever used is secretly a thermodynamic system. When you set temperature=0.7, you're not tweaking a knob — you're fixing the thermal energy of a particle system, frozen at constant heat for the entire generation. That's a problem statistical mechanics solved 150 years ago.

This post walks through Thermostat-LLM: a Physics-Informed Neural Network (PINN) that rides on top of any frozen language model and dynamically controls the sampling temperature at each decoding step — guided entirely by the physics of the token distribution, with no human labels required.

The failure modes nobody talks about

There are exactly two ways LLM generation goes wrong, and they are perfect physical opposites.

❄️

Ordered phase

Solid

The token distribution collapses to near-certainty. Repetition loops, crystallised reasoning chains, re-stating the same premise. Greedy decoding is the extreme case.

💧

Target phase

Liquid

Fluid enough for creativity, structured enough for coherence. The entropy lives in a bounded corridor. This is where good generation lives.

💨

Disordered phase

Gas

The distribution explodes. Sentences drift into semantic noise. Hallucinated facts. Characters who change names mid-paragraph. High temperature causes this.

Current decoding methods — Top-P, Top-K, Min-P, Mirostat — are all heuristics. They were designed by intuition and tuned by feel. None of them have a principled answer to the question: what temperature is correct for this specific context, at this specific step?

Language generation is a physical process. The logical place to control it is with physics.

The physics, for real

This is not a metaphor. The softmax function used in every transformer is a Boltzmann distribution.

When a transformer outputs logits z_i for each token w_i, the sampling probability is:

P(wᵢ) = exp(zᵢ / T) / Σⱼ exp(zⱼ / T)
Temperature-scaled softmax — identical in form to the Boltzmann distribution from statistical mechanics

The statistical mechanics Boltzmann distribution:

P(stateᵢ) = exp(−Eᵢ / k_B T) / Z
The logit zᵢ maps to −Eᵢ. Temperature T has the identical role. Z is the partition function.

The mathematics are exact, not approximate. The token logit is the negative energy of a state. Temperature controls how broadly probability mass is spread. The partition function is just the softmax denominator. Every transformer is already a canonical ensemble — we just haven't been treating it like one.

The order parameter

In thermodynamics, phase transitions are characterised by an order parameter φ — a scalar that tells you which phase a system is in. We define one for token distributions:

φₜ = 1 − Hₜ / H_max
where Hₜ = −Σ P(w) log P(w) is the Shannon entropy of the next-token distribution, and H_max = log V
φ ≈ 0.0 → gas phase (entropy at maximum — all tokens equally likely)
φ ≈ 1.0 → solid phase (entropy near zero — one token dominates)
φ ∈ [0.3, 0.7] → liquid corridor ← our target

When you plot φₜ across decoding steps for a real model, something fascinating appears: natural phase transitions. The order parameter spikes at topic boundaries, dips during factual recall, oscillates during creative passages. The model is already a physical system. The Thermostat just listens to it.

Architecture overview

Thermostat-LLM is a lightweight control layer that wraps any frozen LLM. The backbone never changes; the PINN observes the backbone's internal state and emits a per-step temperature.

What the PINN sees

At each decoding step, the controller receives three signals:

hₜ
Hidden state projection (32-dim)

Last-layer hidden state of the backbone, mean-pooled and projected. Encodes what the model is "thinking about" at this position.

Hₜ
Shannon entropy

How uncertain the model is about the next token. High H → gas phase risk. Low H → solid phase risk.

vₜ
Semantic velocity

Rate of change in embedding space: vₜ = mean(‖hᵢ − hᵢ₋₁‖) over the last 3 tokens. Spikes at topic transitions, falls during repetition.

A GRU gives the controller memory over the sequence — it can "feel" momentum, not just the current snapshot. A small MLP head maps the GRU's hidden state to a scalar Tₜ ∈ [0.1, 2.0] via sigmoid scaling.

The physics loss

The PINN is trained entirely without labels. Physics is the teacher. The loss function penalises violations of three physical laws:

ℒ_phase
Phase containment

Penalises φ leaving the liquid corridor [0.3, 0.7]. Applies a quadratic penalty proportional to how far outside the boundary the order parameter drifts.

0.1 · ℒ_FP
Fokker–Planck smoothness

Penalises large ∂²T/∂t². Temperature must change smoothly — like a physical thermostat. Sudden jumps are forbidden by the diffusion interpretation.

0.5 · ℒ_energy
Gradient direction

When φ < 0.3 (gas), push T down. When φ > 0.7 (solid), push T up. Correct direction of corrective force, weighted by deviation magnitude.

The Fokker–Planck connection

The Fokker–Planck equation describes how a probability density evolves under drift + diffusion: ∂ρ/∂t = −∂/∂x[A(x,t)ρ] + D ∂²ρ/∂x².

In our context, ρ(w, t) is the token distribution, A is the model's natural pull toward high-probability tokens, and D = Tₜ is our diffusion coefficient — the controlled randomness. The smoothness loss directly enforces the physical requirement that a diffusion coefficient must vary continuously.

Interpreting the numbers

The Thermostat achieves zero 3-gram repetition and perfect Distinct-2 diversity on the held-out set — while the fixed-temperature baselines degrade steadily toward greedy. The φ variance tells the more nuanced story: the Thermostat maintains a slightly higher variance because it is actively working, compensating for phase excursions. The baselines have lower variance because they're not correcting — they're just drifting, unchecked, whichever way the distribution happens to go.

Honest caveats

  • Lightly trained. 5 epochs on 10 prompts won't win benchmarks. A production version needs diverse prompt distributions and at least 50–100 training examples, with longer generation windows.
  • No coherence probe. The PINN currently has no signal for factual drift — it can't tell the difference between creative entropy and hallucination entropy. A semantic similarity head (comparing each step's hidden state against the prompt anchor) is the most important next addition.
  • Single scalar temperature. Syntax tokens and semantic tokens have very different optimal temperatures. A per-cluster temperature field would be more expressive — and is directly supported by the Fokker–Planck formalism.
  • GPT-2 ≠ frontier models. The physics is architecture-agnostic, but GPT-2's weaker priors make the distribution harder to control. The same PINN on LLaMA 3 or Mistral would operate from a better foundation.
  • Gas-phase dominance at high T. When the PINN maxes out its temperature range (T → 2.0), it signals that the physics loss has correctly identified a gas-phase excursion but lacks the range to fix it. Extending T_max and adding a stronger phase loss weight would help.

Why this matters

Mirostat, Min-P, Eta Sampling — these are clever heuristics, but they have no principled answer to why a specific temperature is correct for this context. They tune constants by feel and call it a day.

Thermostat-LLM is a step toward principled decoding. By grounding the process in statistical mechanics, three things follow naturally:

Interpretability. You can diagnose failures as "gas phase" or "solid phase" rather than "something went wrong." The order parameter is a readout, not a black box.

Self-supervised training. Physics is the teacher — no human labels, no preference data. The loss function is derived from first principles, not from reward modelling.

Principled generalisation. The Fokker–Planck formalism is not specific to text. The same architecture applies to multi-modal generation — image patches, audio tokens — anywhere a Boltzmann-style sampling process lives in an embedding space.

The softmax always was a Boltzmann distribution. It's time we started treating it like one.

Roadmap

v2
Coherence probe

Adds a factual drift signal: cosine similarity between the current hidden state and the prompt anchor embedding. The PINN gains a semantic compass, not just a thermometer.

v2
Per-cluster temperature

Different temperatures for syntax-heavy tokens vs. semantic tokens, derived from the token's position in embedding cluster space. The FP diffusion coefficient becomes a field, not a scalar.

v3
Renormalisation group decoding

Hierarchical temperature control at sentence, clause, and token scales simultaneously — inspired by RG flow in condensed matter physics. Controls coherence at multiple granularities.

v3
Multi-modal extension

Apply the same Fokker–Planck dynamics in CLIP embedding space for joint text + image generation. Same physics, same controller, different modality.
